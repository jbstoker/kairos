The landscape is the temple.

Frysk Hearren is a sober, land-rooted reference for rebuilding a personal practice from water, stone, tree, soil, and the research trail beneath them.

Read the research Begin at the hearth

There is no central building here. Sacred space begins at a spring, a stone, a tree, a mound, or a hearth. The work is to pay attention, keep the evidence visible, and let the land set the measure.

The home place

A sal built from what is already present.

The physical hearth is a private backyard waterfall and garden in Friesland: water as fontes, a garden stone as petra, and a living tree as nimid. The site is a modern reconstruction grounded in older categories of sacred place.

Privacy boundary. The home sal is not mapped, named by address, or otherwise exposed publicly.

First practice

Offer what the garden gives.

A libation, first fruit, or a moment of stillness at water or stone is enough to begin. No fixed words are required.

The addendum

Follow the evidence back through the layers.

Every entry below signals the kind of claim it makes. These labels are not decoration: they are the standard by which this project judges itself.

c. 1800 BCE–400 CE

Wet places and terpen

Depositions in bogs, pools, and coastal waters, together with ritual evidence from settlement mounds, form the oldest named-less layer.

Archaeology · attested

AD 28

Baduhenna’s grove

Tacitus records a sacred grove of Baduhenna in connection with a Frisian victory over Roman troops. Its location is not securely known.

Existence attested · location inferred

1st century CE

Fosite and Hludana

Fosite’s Helgoland sanctuary and the votive stone for Hludana preserve rare named points in the record.

Attested

8th century

Groves, stones, springs, fire

The Indiculus prohibits rites at groves, stones, springs, and the friction-made need-fire. It is a prohibition list, not a ritual handbook.

Source attested

17th–19th centuries

Survival and overlay

Folklore and Christian feast days can preserve older patterns, but only where they converge with earlier evidence.

Folklore · requires comparison

Present day

Careful reconstruction

Contemporary practice may be useful, but it must never be passed off as an inherited certainty.
