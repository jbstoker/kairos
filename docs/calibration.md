# Kairos Calibration Guide

Kairos is only as good as your observations. This guide explains how to make
each one with tools you already have.

## Solar noon (the core anchor)

Solar noon is when the sun crosses your local meridian — the moment your
shadow is shortest and points due north (southern hemisphere: due south).
The web app offers two easy ways to record it:

- **🌅 Sunrise + 🌇 Sunset** — record both; the midpoint is solar noon.
- **⚖️ Shadow = Stick** — press when a stick's shadow equals the stick,
  once in the morning and once in the afternoon; the midpoint is noon.

### Method 1 — gnomon (recommended)

1. Plant a straight stick vertically in flat ground.
2. Mark the tip of its shadow every few minutes around midday.
3. The shortest shadow *is* solar noon. Press the button / record it.

The shadow changes slowly near noon, so a day of practice beats a single
reading. A few noons in a row will give you a very good anchor.

### Method 2 — rising / setting

If you miss noon, note the times of sunrise and sunset; the midpoint is
close to solar noon (adjusted for the equation of time).

### Method 3 — equal shadows (fallback)

A stick's shadow equals its height when the sun is at 45° altitude — twice
each day, once in the morning and once in the afternoon. Record both
moments; their midpoint is solar noon. This is the web app's
"⚖️ Shadow = Stick" button.

### Notes

- Kairos time is *local solar time*: noon is when the sun is highest, not
  when your clock says 12:00.
- During daylight saving, your wall clock may disagree by an hour. That's
  fine — Kairos trusts the sun, not the clock.

## Moon phase

Look at the moon and pick the closest of the eight phases:

    🌑 new    🌒 waxing crescent   🌓 first quarter   🌔 waxing gibbous
    🌕 full   🌖 waning gibbous   🌗 last quarter    🌘 waning crescent

The moon is at first quarter / last quarter when exactly half is lit, and
full/new when it looks like a disk / nothing. "Waxing" = growing brighter;
"waning" = shrinking.

## Season events

Choose the event that best marks the season where *you* live:

- **Spring** — first leaf buds, first flowers, thaw
- **Summer** — longest days, first heat, full canopy
- **Autumn** — first frost, leaves turning, harvest
- **Winter** — first lasting snow, shortest days

You are the authority. If spring arrives in September where you live, record
it then. Kairos has no opinion about the hemisphere you happen to stand on.

## Keeping a good observation log

- Record solar noon at least once every couple of weeks (drift is small,
  but weather happens).
- Update the moon phase whenever it visibly changes — about every 3-4 days.
- Update the season when the landscape tells you to.
